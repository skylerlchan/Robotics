What changed: Rev E is one part, repeated

The previous segment had eleven parts, a chip and two locks each. Rev E throws that away. A tendril is fourteen identical moulded beads threaded on a 1.4 mm superelastic nitinol rod, three cables through the beads to a motor cartridge at the base, and one vacuum sleeve over the outside. Nothing electronic lives in the tendril. Shape is known from the cable lengths at the spools, force from the cable tensions, contact from a camera in the base looking up and the skin only at the tip.

How it holds itself, in three regimes, all measured below. The rod is the skeleton: it carries the tendril's own weight with almost no sag and spreads any bend evenly along the length. The cables are the muscle: pull one and the rod bends toward it, hold the cable and the shape holds. The sleeve is the brake: pull a vacuum and thin polymer layers inside it jam together, so the tendril keeps its shape with the cables slack and the motors off. Holding a wrap costs nothing because the spool motors are worm drives that cannot be back-driven.

Strength comes from bundling and wrapping, not from a fatter cable. Wrap a bar and the load goes into cable tension and friction, where the chain is strongest.

What this shows and what it does not

It shows that a chain of moulded beads on a spring rod, driven by three cables and a vacuum sleeve, holds itself and a load, places its tip within a few millimetres, pinches, wraps, cages, threads and acts in parallel, with forces and stiffnesses taken from real materials.
It does not show learned behaviour. Every controller is a script written for its scene. The next step is exactly what LIBERO and RoboCasa exist for: wrap this model as an environment, collect demonstrations, and train a policy that produces these actions from observations.

Known limits of the current model, stated so they can be fixed in order. Eight tendrils of 168 mm stand in for twenty-four of 220 mm. Each tendril is one bending section, so it can wrap but cannot lift a wrapped object without the base moving; a second cable set ending mid-length fixes that. The jamming stiffness is set at fifteen times the rod, a literature figure, not a measured one. Bundling by twisting tendrils together is not yet scripted. And the parallel task went through three versions before it passed: dragging cubes along the floor failed because the tips plough against floor friction, toppling pegs failed because the descending arc pins them instead of striking them, and the version kept here hovers eight tips over eight pads. All three are recorded rather than hidden.
